# TEN-STREAM RESEARCH SYNTHESIS

Ten parallel research streams, ~25 distinct methods, ~110 method-evaluations.
Run 2026-07-28. Every stream was instructed to discount affiliate-monetized
and course-selling sources, separate *"someone made $X"* from *"a typical
committed person makes $X"*, and report **evidence: weak** rather than
invent a number.

**Profile researched against:** strong writing/copy; cannot code; cannot edit
video; will do phone/video calls but not in-person sales; 10–15 hrs/week;
~$300 upfront plus subscriptions if justified; target **$200–1,000/mo net**;
first revenue inside 60–90 days; US-based.

---

## 0. THE CONTRADICTION — RESOLVED

Two streams covered Outlier (Scale AI) and reached opposite verdicts. This
matters more than any ranking below, because acting on the wrong one costs
real unpaid work.

| | Stream 6 (AI training) | Stream 10 (remote gig) |
|---|---|---|
| Verdict | MARGINAL — "apply as a hedge" | **AVOID outright** |
| Payment | "well-attested… never had issues paying on time" | Documented mass non-payment |
| Sourcing | Self-described as **Reddit-derived, "could not verify at source"** | Inc. investigative series, AlgorithmWatch, active petition |

**Stream 10 wins, decisively, on evidence:**

- **AlgorithmWatch** — 200+ workers contacted, 50+ in-depth interviews, four
  continents: **~34% of platform work time is unpaid** (avg 7.8 unpaid of
  22.7 weekly hours). A US-based Outlier worker reported effective earnings
  **under $5/hour**. 500+ workers terminated Oct 2024 without the required
  60-day notice.
- **Inc. Magazine** (Sam Blum) — sustained investigation: mass non-payment
  accusations, a class action alleging an "extremely predatory" contractor
  relationship, 45 former contractors writing to Senate Commerce alleging
  wage theft.
- **Coworker.org petition** — demanding Scale/Outlier stop withholding earned
  wages, passed 1,000 signatures **2026-07-22, six days before this
  research**. Describes accounts "closed with no warning, no explanation,
  and no appeal process," balances withheld.
- **US DOL opened an FLSA investigation into Scale AI, then dropped it** —
  the subpoena will not be enforced. The regulatory backstop weakened rather
  than strengthened.

**Do not use Outlier or Remotasks.** Stream 6's own separate AVOID calls on
**Handshake AI** (Business Insider, March 2026: five contractors suspended,
four unpaid, two lawsuits, one ruling that $6,475 was owed) and **Alignerr**
(community sentiment 38/100, confirmed unpaid-work reports, silent account
deletions) stand and are corroborated in shape by the above.

**The generalizable lesson:** in this sector the advertised rate is inversely
correlated with payment reliability. The high rates are the bait. Wherever
you do this work, **cash out at the lowest available threshold, every single
week.** Frozen pending balances — not low rates — are the primary loss
mechanism across every platform in the category.

## 1. THE CONVERGENCE

Five of ten streams — assigned different clusters, given no knowledge of each
other — independently arrived at the same two conclusions:

1. **Sell the writing directly.** Every cluster built on *volume* of
   undifferentiated output (stock, POD, printables, faceless video, niche
   sites) has been repriced to near zero by AI. Every cluster built on
   *a specific human doing a specific thing for a specific buyer* held its
   price.
2. **A niche newsletter is the right compounding asset** — though never the
   90-day answer.

Convergence from independent priors is the strongest signal in this
document. It is worth more than any individual number in it.

**The structural explanation**, stated by three streams in near-identical
terms: AI collapsed the cost of *building and producing*, simultaneously, for
everyone. So production stopped being scarce and **distribution became the
entire bottleneck.** Writing is a distribution skill. Building software,
generating images, and assembling video are production skills. Pairing a
strong distribution skill with a weak production skill by choosing a
production business is backwards.

## 2. WHAT THE LOWER TARGET CHANGES

At $3,000/mo you need leverage, which forces you into selling services at
high prices with all the sales risk that implies. At **$200–1,000/mo with
10–15 hrs/week, a boring hourly job at $19–25/hr solves the entire problem**
in 10–12 hours a week with near-zero variance.

That reframing is why the ranking below looks nothing like a hustle blog.
Speculative plays have to *beat* a reliable $780/mo, not merely be exciting.
Almost none of them do.

## 4. THE TEN NUMBERS THAT DECIDED IT

1. **68,000 stock assets → $857/month**, declining 30%/yr. Your 300 AI images
   would make ~$10/mo. Adobe now caps submissions by performance and bans
   >3 prompt iterations — the volume strategy is a written policy violation.
2. **Etsy: 5.6M sellers, $10.46B GMS = ~$156/mo gross for the mean seller**,
   median likely $20–35. Sellers +3.3%, buyers −2.1%, take rate up to 24.2%.
   (SEC 10-K — the best primary source in the project.)
3. **Google AI answers cut publisher clicks 40–58%.** Pew tracked 68,000 real
   queries: click rate 15% → 8%. Users click links *inside* AI summaries
   **1% of the time** — citation is not traffic.
4. **TikTok Shop: the top 20% of affiliates earn ~$87/month.**
5. **10.3% of apps in Lovable's own showcase** had database tables readable
   by unauthenticated requests. 45% of AI-generated code fails OWASP
   benchmarks. SaaStr — a technical team — burned $8,000/mo on a vibe-coded
   project that never shipped.
6. **Median SaaS acquisition multiple: 3.9x annual profit.** A genuine
   $200/mo app costs ~$9,400. $300 buys ~$6/month of profit.
7. **Domain flipping sell-through is 1–1.5%/year.** $300 buys ~27 domains =
   expected **0.3 sales** in year one, plus $300 in renewals.
8. **Upwork reply rates: copywriting 14.24%, AI/ML 7.21%.** Demand for
   writing fell; supply of bidders fled faster.
9. **EFA 2026 rate chart** (n=1,100+, fielded Nov 2025–Jan 2026): copyediting
   $40–50/hr, academic $50–60/hr, medical $60–75/hr. **No AI-editing
   category exists yet** — the market is real but unformalized, so you set
   the price.
10. **Newsletters trade at 30–45x monthly net profit.** A newsletter netting
    $400/mo is a $12,000–18,000 saleable asset.

## 5. HOW TO SPOT THE FRAUD

Patterns that recurred across all ten streams:

- **When practitioners are leaving and marketers are recruiting, believe the
  practitioners.** Working stock contributors publish declining numbers and
  pivot away; the people publishing growth claims sell keywording tools.
- **Search-result composition is itself evidence.** "Merch by Amazon 2026"
  returned 7 Gumroad course pages out of 10. Every page-one result for
  faceless-YouTube earnings was owned by a faceless-video tool vendor.
- **Recursive income reports.** The highest-earning "how much I earn on
  Medium" writers earn primarily *from writing about earning on Medium*.
  Real numbers, completely non-transferable.
- **Stale data in fresh packaging.** The "70% of Fiverr sellers make under
  $100/mo" stat is from ~2017. The Quora Partner Program died in 2023 and is
  still being sold.
- **Manufactured compliance panic.** Multiple vendors selling AI-compliance
  tools assert Etsy imposed a 2026 AI-disclosure rule. Etsy's SEC filings —
  legally required to disclose material policy matters — contain no such
  thing.
- **Affiliate conflicts to know:** GoHighLevel pays ~40% recurring (hence
  every "start an AI agency" video); micro1 pays **$3,000 per referral**
  (hence every positive micro1 review).
- **Multiplying a price by an imagined client count is not a business model.**
- **Any upfront-fee certification.** Proofreading, transcription, bookkeeping,
  and "AI consultant" credentials are all sold, none required. QuickBooks
  ProAdvisor is free.

## 6. EVIDENCE LIMITATIONS — STATED PLAINLY

- **Reddit was inaccessible to every stream** (403 to the crawler). It was
  the brief's top-priority source for "what a typical committed person
  actually earns." Its absence is the single biggest gap in this project.
  30 minutes of manual reading in r/WorkOnline, r/freelanceWriters, and
  r/Flipping would materially improve Tiers 1 and 3.
- **The shared web-search budget was exhausted**, so the last three streams
  are thinner. Weakest coverage: local-city newsletters, AI-literacy
  training pricing, mock-interview platforms, small-newsletter ad revenue.
- **Almost every "% who reach $200/mo" figure is reasoned inference, not
  measurement.** No platform publishes per-seller income distributions.
  Anyone quoting a precise figure is guessing or selling.
- **The negative findings are far better evidenced than the positive ones.**
  Traffic collapse, commission cuts, threshold increases, non-payment
  patterns, and platform policy are documented from primary sources. The
  case *for* newsletters rests partly on CPM data of only moderate quality.
  Treat the AVOID list as near-certain and the Tier 1/2 upside as the best
  available reasoning.
- **Unverified, flagged by the streams themselves:** Shutterstock's
  contributor AI-submission policy (could not retrieve; penalty for guessing
  wrong is account termination); Wyzant's current platform fee; whether
  Respondent still deducts a processing fee.
