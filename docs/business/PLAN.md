# THE PLAN — $200–1,000/MO AT 10–15 HRS/WEEK

Derived from `RESEARCH.md` (ten research streams, ~25 methods). Scoped to:
strong writer, no code, no video, will take calls, ~$300, 60–90 days.

**Total spend to execute this plan: ~$20/month.** The $300 is not needed.
Hold it.

---

## THE SHAPE

Three layers, started in parallel in week 1. Not three plans — one plan.

| Layer | Purpose | Pays |
|---|---|---|
| **Floor** | Kills the money problem outright | $600–850/mo, reliable |
| **Escalator** | Raises your hourly over time | $40 → $60/hr |
| **Asset** | Compounds; sellable later | $0 now, $12–18k later |

Most people pick one and it's the wrong one. The floor makes you calm enough
to build the escalator; the escalator funds the asset; the asset is the only
thing here you own.

## LAYER 1 — THE FLOOR (weeks 1–6)

**Remote part-time customer support — chat/email, W-2 preferred.**

12 hrs/wk × $19/hr ≈ **$990 gross, ~$780 net**, on scheduled shifts. That
single line satisfies the entire stated target with near-zero variance.

- [ ] Apply to **20–30 roles.** Volume is the whole game; the funnel is real.
- [ ] Prioritize **chat and email over phone queues** — better pay, and
      strong writing is a genuine differentiator in ticket-based support.
- [ ] Prioritize **W-2 over per-talk-minute IC.** IC programs (Liveops-style)
      leave you unpaid while idle; realized rate drops to $9–15/hr.
- [ ] Expect: apply → interview → background check → 1–2 weeks training →
      first pay cycle. **Earning by week 4–6.**

Employers: Teleperformance, TTEC, Concentrix, Working Solutions, plus direct
SaaS/e-commerce postings. BLS puts the median at $20.59/hr with ~341,700
openings annually — headcount is declining but replacement churn keeps
hiring constant.

**Why this and not something more interesting:** at a $200–1,000 target,
every speculative play has to beat a reliable $780/mo *after* accounting for
the months it earns nothing. Almost none do.

## LAYER 2 — THE ESCALATOR (weeks 2–12)

**"AI draft → publishable copy" editing, sold as a named service.**

This is the highest-leverage positioning available to you. Not "proofreader"
— that's a $5 Fiverr commodity. **AI-output editor.** The buyer is an SMB or
agency sitting on LLM drafts that read plausibly but are wrong, repetitive,
and voiceless. Volume of published text exploded; median quality collapsed.

- [ ] Price off the **EFA 2026 rate chart** (n=1,100+, fielded Nov 2025–Jan
      2026), never off Fiverr: copyediting **$40–50/hr**, academic **$50–60**,
      medical **$60–75**.
- [ ] Pitch: agencies, SMB marketing leads, indie authors, academics.
- [ ] Job sources: Reedsy, ACES and EFA job lists, academic editing firms,
      agency subcontracting.
- [ ] **Do not buy a proofreading certification.** $500–1,200 courses exist;
      no client or employer requires one. Pure info-product funnel.
- [ ] EFA membership (~$165/yr) is optional — only once you're billing.

Notably, **the 2026 EFA chart still has no AI-editing category.** The market
exists but isn't formalized, which means you set the price rather than
discovering it.

Structural advantage: there is no platform here. Nobody can deactivate you,
change a take rate, or freeze a balance. Client relationships are yours.

### Parallel fast-cash: resume + LinkedIn packages

$150–700 per package, ~3 hours each. **One package clears the $200 target.**
A 30-minute intake call is what separates a $400 resume from a $60 one — and
most writers won't do calls. Sell it as a productized flat fee.

### Parallel: Upwork, bidding into the arbitrage

From 133,872 analyzed proposals: **Sales & Marketing Copywriting replies at
14.24%; AI & Machine Learning at 7.21%**, against a 7.45% platform mean.
Everyone stampeded into "AI." Bid where you can actually deliver.

- [ ] Bid **copywriting and marketing/PR**, explicitly not AI/ML.
- [ ] Target clients with **$1–5K lifetime platform spend** (8.15% reply),
      not the $500K+ whales everyone chases (3.85%).
- [ ] Budget $20–60/mo in Connects. Take every discovery call offered.
- [ ] Survive the 4–10 week silence. That phase is where nearly everyone
      quits, and quitting is the actual failure mode — not skill.

## LAYER 3 — THE ASSET (weeks 4+, ~3 hrs/week)

**A weekly niche newsletter.** Three independent streams converged on this.

Be clear-eyed: **9–18 months to $200/mo.** It is not the 90-day answer and
anyone selling it as one is lying. It's here because it's the only thing in
the entire study that *appreciates*:

- You own the list. No algorithm, no take rate, no deactivation.
- Email is not indexed, so AI search didn't touch it.
- It gets **more** valuable as AI floods the zone with generic content.
- **Newsletters trade at 30–45x monthly net profit.** One netting $400/mo is
  a $12,000–18,000 saleable asset.
- Your willingness to take calls is the rare input — most newsletter writers
  won't pitch advertisers, and direct sponsorship is a calls business.

### Niche choice dominates everything else

This single decision is worth more than a year of execution:

| | B2B / tech / finance | Consumer / lifestyle |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsorship CPM | $60–180 | $15–50 |
| Paid conversion | up to 20% (finance top decile) | 0.62% median |
| Subscription price | $27/mo (investing) | $7/mo (travel) |

Combined, that swings the list size needed for $200/mo by **more than 20x** —
roughly 4,000 subscribers for general lifestyle versus a few hundred for a
tight professional niche.

- [ ] Pick a narrow professional vertical where you have real knowledge.
- [ ] Publish weekly. Monetize with **direct-sold sponsorships first**
      ($100–250 introductory placement at ~500 subs), paid tier second.
- [ ] Original reporting via calls is the differentiator AI cannot fake.
- [ ] Platform: beehiiv or Substack. Free until you charge.
- [ ] CAN-SPAM: valid physical postal address in every send. Get a PO box or
      virtual mailbox — don't publish your home address.

## SUPPLEMENTS — SIGN UP WEEK 1, COUNT ON NOTHING

Free, fast, honest. **$80–250/mo of background ballast.** All three are
invitation-gated, not effort-gated — you cannot make them pay more by working
harder, which is exactly why they can't be the plan.

- [ ] **Prolific** — first dollar in 1–7 days, fastest here. Fill every
      demographic screener completely; it directly determines invite volume.
- [ ] **Respondent** — $75 average per session, but their own site concedes
      "most participants get 1–3 invitations a month." Never misrepresent
      your job title to qualify.
- [ ] **UserTesting** — think-aloud narration; clear speakers get invited
      back. Payment lands 14 days after each test.
- [ ] **DataAnnotation.tech** — the one AI-training platform worth using.
      Writing judgment is the product; realized $18–22/hr. **Take the
      assessment slowly and sober, and do not use AI to write your answers —
      that's a documented auto-fail.**

## DO NOT TOUCH

**Platforms with documented non-payment:** Outlier / Scale AI, Handshake AI,
Alignerr, Remotasks. See `RESEARCH.md` §0 — this is the most decision-relevant
finding in the study. The advertised rate is the bait.

**Anywhere you do this work: cash out at the lowest threshold, every week.**
Frozen pending balances, not low rates, are how people lose money here.

**Any upfront-fee certification.** Proofreading, transcription, bookkeeping,
"AI consultant." None are required by any client or employer. QuickBooks
ProAdvisor is free.

**The whole Tier 4 list** in `RESEARCH.md` §3 — 45+ methods, each with a
documented reason.

## KILL CRITERIA — SET NOW, WHILE IT'S CHEAP

- **Support roles:** 30 applications, no interview → the résumé is the
  problem, not the market. Rewrite it (you can do this).
- **Editing:** 40 pitches, no client → reposition from "editing" to a
  narrower vertical. Don't lower the rate.
- **Upwork:** 40 proposals, under 3% reply → wrong category or wrong client
  size. Re-read the arbitrage table above.
- **Newsletter:** 12 weekly issues, under 100 subscribers → wrong niche.
  Change the niche, not the cadence.

The dominant failure mode across every stream was not picking wrong. It was
**stopping in week 3**, before the funnel had produced enough data to say
anything. Set the thresholds now so the decision isn't made on a bad Tuesday.
