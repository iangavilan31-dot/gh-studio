# DELIVERY — BUILD AND ONBOARDING CHECKLIST

What you actually do after someone pays. No code. Everything here is
configuration in GoHighLevel (Starter, $97/mo) plus SMS usage.

Target: **client live within 5 business days of payment.**

---

## 0. THE DEMO — BUILD THIS BEFORE YOU SELL ANYTHING

In the free trial, using a fake business ("Northside Plumbing"):

- [ ] A phone number that forwards to your cell
- [ ] Missed-call text-back triggered on unanswered
- [ ] A two-message review request sequence
- [ ] **A 90-second screen recording** — you call the number, don't answer,
      the text arrives on camera

That recording is the highest-leverage asset in this entire business.
Owners do not buy a description of automation. They buy watching a phone
buzz. Record it once, send it a hundred times.

Do not perfect this. Good enough to record is good enough to ship.

---

## 1. ONBOARDING — DAY 1

One 30-minute call. Collect everything at once; chasing details over a week
is how a 5-day build becomes a 3-week build.

- [ ] Business name exactly as it appears on Google
- [ ] Main phone number and who currently answers it
- [ ] Google Business Profile access (manager, not owner — ask for manager)
- [ ] Average job value *(you need this for the results report)*
- [ ] Hours, service area, trades they do and don't do
- [ ] Their three most common customer questions
- [ ] Where completed jobs get recorded — a CRM, a whiteboard, a notebook.
      Usually a notebook. Plan for that.

### Screenshot the baseline. Do not skip this.

- [ ] Current Google review count
- [ ] Current star average
- [ ] Date, visible in the screenshot

Without a before, there is no after, and the case study is the real product
of your first engagement. This takes 60 seconds and you cannot recover it
later.

### Set expectations out loud

- Reviews build over weeks, not days. First new ones in ~2 weeks.
- Missed-call text-back produces results immediately.
- They approve every message before anything sends.
- What you will not do, and why — see `COMPLIANCE.md` §1.

## 2. BUILD — DAYS 2–4

### 2.1 Number and routing

- [ ] Provision the number
- [ ] Forward to their existing line so nothing changes for them
- [ ] Register the A2P campaign under your sole-prop brand
- [ ] Test: call it, let it ring out, confirm the text lands

### 2.2 Missed-call text-back

Fires within ~5 seconds of an unanswered call.

```
Sorry we missed you — this is [Business Name]. What do you need done?
We'll get right back to you.

Reply STOP to opt out.
```

- [ ] Replies route somewhere the owner will actually see them. Ask which
      app they already have open all day and use that. Do not introduce a
      new one.
- [ ] If unanswered by the owner for 30 minutes, notify them again.
- [ ] Business-hours logic — after-hours gets a different message with the
      next open time.

### 2.3 Review request

Triggered on job completion. Whatever "completion" means in their world:
a tag, a pipeline stage, or a text from the owner saying "done."

Make it as close to zero-effort as possible. If marking a job complete
takes more than one tap, it will not happen and the whole review half of
your product quietly dies.

**Message 1 — 2 hours after completion:**

```
Hi [Name], thanks for choosing [Business Name] today. If you have a
minute, a quick Google review really helps us out: [link]

Reply STOP to opt out.
```

**Message 2 — 3 days later, only if no review detected:**

```
Hi [Name], just following up — if you have a moment for a quick review
we'd appreciate it: [link] Either way, thanks for your business.

Reply STOP to opt out.
```

Two messages. Never a third.

**Hard constraints, non-negotiable:**

- Same message to **every** completed job. No filtering by perceived
  sentiment.
- No incentive of any kind mentioned. Not "we'd love your feedback and
  here's 10% off." Nothing.
- Direct link to the public Google review form. No intermediate survey, no
  "how did we do?" star-picker that routes low scores elsewhere. That is
  review gating and it is a policy violation.

Build it so filtering is *technically impossible*. Then a busy week can't
drift you into a violation.

### 2.4 Approval

- [ ] Send every message to the owner for sign-off before going live
- [ ] Get it in writing (email is fine)

They will change a word. Let them — it makes it theirs, and an owner who
edited the copy defends the service internally.

## 3. LAUNCH — DAY 5

- [ ] Live
- [ ] Test end-to-end one more time from a phone that isn't yours
- [ ] Text the owner: *"You're live. Miss a call on purpose and watch what
      happens."* They always do it. It's the moment the service becomes
      real to them.

---

## 4. ONGOING — ~2 HOURS/MONTH PER CLIENT

Weekly:
- [ ] Check for stuck conversations or failed sends
- [ ] Confirm review requests are actually firing (if job-completion
      marking has silently stopped, this is where you catch it)

Monthly — **the results email.** This is what renews the retainer:

```
[Month] — [Business Name]

Missed calls recovered:     [N]
Conversations started:      [N]
Estimated value recovered:  [N × their average ticket]

Google reviews:  [before] → [after]
Star average:    [before] → [after]

[One sentence on anything you changed or noticed.]
```

Send it on the 1st, every month, without being asked. Most agencies go
silent between invoices, and the contrast is most of why clients stay.

## 5. CAPACITY

| Clients | Delivery load | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | ~6 hrs/mo | Nearly all your time still goes to selling |
| 4–6 | ~12 hrs/mo | Comfortable solo |
| 7–8 | ~16 hrs/mo | Selling time starts getting squeezed |
| 9+ | — | Raise prices or get help. Do not just absorb it. |

The failure mode past eight is not being overworked — it's that delivery
quietly eats the outreach hours, the pipeline empties, and two months later
a churned client has no replacement. Protect the selling time.
