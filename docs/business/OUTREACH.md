# OUTREACH — COPY AND SCRIPTS

Everything you send or say, ready to use. Replace bracketed tokens.
Compliance rules referenced here are enforced in `COMPLIANCE.md`.

---

## 0. THE ONE RULE

Every message answers *"why are you emailing me specifically"* in the first
sentence, or it gets deleted. Personalization is not the first name. It is
one observed fact that could only be true of that business.

You have a real one available to you and almost nobody uses it: **call them
first, let it go to voicemail, then email about the call you just made.**
It's true, it's specific, and it demonstrates the problem instead of
describing it.

---

## 1. COLD EMAIL SEQUENCE

Four emails over 12 days. Stop the sequence the moment anyone replies.

Every email carries the CAN-SPAM footer. No exceptions, including follow-ups.

```
--
[Your Name] · [Business Name]
[Phone]
[Physical postal address — street, PO Box, or CMRA mailbox]
Don't want to hear from me? Reply "stop" and I'll remove you immediately.
```

---

### Email 1 — Day 0

**Subject:** `quick question about [Business Name]`

```
Hi [First Name],

I called your office Tuesday around 2pm and got voicemail. No criticism —
you were almost certainly under someone's sink.

That's actually what I want to talk about. For a [plumber/roofer/HVAC
company] running [$400]+ average tickets, a missed call that never gets
returned usually isn't a lost call. It's a booked job that went to whoever
picked up second.

I set up an automatic text that fires the second a call goes unanswered:
"Sorry we missed you — this is [Business Name], what do you need done?"
The customer texts back, you reply when you're off the ladder, the job
stays yours.

Worth 10 minutes to look at what yours would recover?

[Your Name]
[Phone]
```

Why it works: verifiable, specific, and the mechanism is explained in one
sentence with no jargon. No "AI," no "automation solutions," no "digital
transformation." Owners in the trades hear those words and hang up.

---

### Email 2 — Day 3

**Subject:** `re: quick question about [Business Name]`

```
[First Name] —

Following up once with the actual math, because it's the part that
surprises people.

Say you miss 3 calls a day. Two of those callers phone someone else and
never call back. At a [$400] ticket that's [$800] a day walking out the
door — [$4,000] a week, not counting the reviews and referrals those jobs
would have produced.

You're already paying for those calls. Marketing, trucks, the sign. The
only leak is the seconds between the call and the callback.

I have a 90-second recording of what this looks like on a real phone.
Want me to send it?

[Your Name]
```

Adjust ticket size to the trade. Roofing and HVAC replacement run far higher
than $400 — use their number, and lowball it. An owner who thinks your figure
is inflated stops reading; one who thinks it's conservative does the math
himself and arrives at a bigger number than you would have dared claim.

---

### Email 3 — Day 7

**Subject:** `your reviews vs [Competitor Name]`

```
[First Name],

Unrelated to the missed calls, but I noticed it while I was looking:
[Competitor] has [X] Google reviews. You have [Y], and your most recent
one is from [Month].

You're not doing worse work than them. You're just not asking. They almost
certainly automated the ask and you haven't.

The second half of what I do is a text that goes to every customer after a
completed job — same message to everyone, no discounts or gift cards for
reviews (that's banned and it gets profiles suspended), just a plain ask
with a direct link.

Businesses go from a handful of reviews to 40 or 50 in a few months on
nothing but consistency.

Ten minutes this week?

[Your Name]
```

Pull the real competitor and the real numbers. It takes 30 seconds in Google
Maps and it is the single most-replied-to email in this sequence, because it
is the one that makes them feel something.

Note that you flag the incentive ban *in the pitch*. It positions you as the
person who knows the rules, and it pre-empts the "my nephew said to offer $10
off" conversation before it starts.

---

### Email 4 — Day 12 (breakup)

**Subject:** `closing this out`

```
[First Name] —

I'll stop here, I don't want to be the guy who emails five times.

If missed calls and reviews ever move up your list, my number's below and
I'll pick up.

Good luck with the season.

[Your Name]
```

Short, no guilt, no fake scarcity. The breakup email gets a startling
reply rate precisely because it asks for nothing.

## 2. PHONE

### 2.1 Follow-up call — 48h after email 1

They almost certainly haven't read it. That's fine; the email buys you the
right to reference something.

```
"Hi, is this [First Name]? — [Your Name], I sent you a note a couple days
back about missed calls, figured I'd just call instead of emailing again.

Have I caught you at a bad time?"
```

**Wait for the answer.** If it's bad, ask when's better and hang up. That
single question does more for your close rate than any pitch, because the
trades get called by vendors twelve times a day and nobody ever asks.

If it's fine:

```
"Thirty seconds and you can tell me to get lost. When you're on a job and
a call comes in that you can't take — what happens to that person?"
```

Then stop talking. The answer is always some version of *"they leave a
voicemail, I call back when I can."* That is the entire opening, and they
opened it themselves.

```
"Right. And realistically, how often do you get to it same day?"

"That's what I fix. Call goes unanswered, they get a text within about
five seconds, and about half of them text back — because they'll text
when they won't leave a voicemail. Job stays yours instead of going to
whoever answers next.

Can I show you? Fifteen minutes, I'll screen-share, and if it's not
obvious inside five you can kick me off the call."
```

### 2.2 Discovery call — 15 minutes

**Do not present for the first ten.** Ask:

1. "How do most of your jobs come in — phone, website, referral?"
2. "Roughly how many calls a day?"
3. "Who answers when you're on a job?"
4. "How many of those do you think you're missing?" *(they will guess low
   — it's fine, use their number)*
5. "What's an average job worth to you?"
6. "How are you getting Google reviews right now?"

Question 4 × question 5 is the whole sale, and **they** computed it. Say it
back to them once, flat, no emphasis:

```
"So on your own numbers — three a day, [$400] a job, and half of those
never call back — that's around [$600] a day. I'm going to be $400 a
month."
```

Then be quiet. Whoever speaks first loses, and this is the one moment in the
call where that cliché is literally true.

### 2.3 The close

```
"Here's how it works. $500 to set it up, $400 a month after that. I
build it this week, you approve every message before anything sends,
and if you hate it in month one, you cancel and keep the setup.

Want me to start?"
```

Month-to-month, no contract. You are selling to people who have been burned
by a marketing agency before — all of them have — and a 12-month contract is
the exact shape of that memory.

## 3. OBJECTIONS

**"I already have someone doing my marketing."**
> "Good, I'm not trying to replace them. They're filling the top of your
> funnel — I'm plugging a leak at the bottom. What happens to the calls
> they generate when you can't pick up?"

**"$400 a month is a lot."**
> "It is, if it doesn't work. One recovered job pays for the month. If it
> doesn't recover you at least one job a month, you should cancel — I'd
> tell you to."

**"I answer all my calls."**
> "Then you're better than most. Can I ask — when you're in a crawlspace
> or on a roof, does the phone come with you?" *(Nobody says yes. They
> laugh, and that laugh is the sale.)*

**"Just email me some information."**
> "Happy to. What specifically do you want me to answer in it, so I'm not
> sending you a brochure?" *(Either you get a real question and a real
> next step, or you find out it's a brush-off and stop spending time.)*

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Of course. What's the part you're unsure about — whether it works, or
> whether it's worth $400?" *(These are completely different objections and
> you cannot handle either one until you know which it is.)*

**"Can you get me reviews?"**
> "I can get you *real* ones, consistently, from customers you already
> served. What I won't do is buy them or offer people discounts for them —
> that's banned, and the fines run past $50,000 per review. The good news
> is you don't need any of that. You just need to ask every single
> customer, every time, and never miss one."

That last answer wins deals. Every owner in the trades has been pitched fake
reviews by somebody. Being the person who explains the rule — and still
solves the problem — is a credential nobody else in their inbox has.

## 4. VOLUME AND CADENCE

| Week | Emails/day | Calls/day |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 (warming) | 0 |
| 2 | 20 | 5 |
| 3+ | 30–50 | 15–20 |

Never exceed ~50/day on one domain. Send Tuesday–Thursday. Call
7:30–9am or 4–6pm — before the first job or after the last one.

Track only four numbers: **sent → replied → called → closed.** If replies
are under 3%, the list is wrong. If calls-to-closed is under 1 in 5, the
offer or the price is wrong. Everything else is vanity.
