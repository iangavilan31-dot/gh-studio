## 0. THE OFFER, IN ONE SENTENCE

> "You missed 40 calls last month. Each one was worth about $400. I make sure
> none of them go unanswered, and I turn the jobs you win into Google reviews."

Two mechanics, one product:

1. **Missed-call text-back.** Call goes unanswered → automatic SMS within
   seconds → conversation → booked job.
2. **Compliant review generation.** Job marked complete → neutral review
   request → Google Business Profile.

Sold together because #1 creates the revenue that justifies the fee, and #2
creates the visible proof the owner brags about. #1 pays for you. #2 keeps you.

## 2. PRICING

**$500 setup + $400/month.**

The setup fee is not decoration. It:
- covers your tool costs the moment a client signs, so you never float them
- filters tire-kickers, who are the entire cost of this business model
- makes the monthly feel small by comparison

Discount the *setup* if you must. **Never discount the monthly** — it resets
your price anchor for every future client and they talk to each other.

Client #1 only: setup fee waived in exchange for a written testimonial and
permission to use their numbers. Say explicitly that it's a founding-client
rate. Never repeat it.

### Revenue shape

| Clients | Monthly | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $400 | Case study. The real deliverable. |
| 3 | $1,200 | Covers tools with room left. |
| 5 | $2,000 | Realistic 90-day target. |

Treat "5 clients at 90 days" as success. The $10k/month figures in agency
blogs are the product being sold to you, not a forecast.

## 3. THE EIN TRAP — READ BEFORE REGISTERING ANYTHING

Sending business SMS in the US requires A2P 10DLC carrier registration. There
are three brand tiers, and which one you land in is decided by **whether you
have a business tax ID**:

| Tier | Requires | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Sole Proprietor** | **No** EIN / tax ID | ~$4 brand + $15 campaign vetting + $2/mo |
| Low Volume Standard | EIN, under 6,000 segments/day | Higher fees, more vetting |
| Standard | EIN, over 6,000 segments/day | Highest |

Sole Proprietor registration is **only available to people without a business
tax ID**. If you have an EIN you are ineligible and must register as a
Standard brand.

**So: do not get an EIN yet.** Register the Sole Proprietor brand first, at
$4, this week. GoHighLevel supports sole-prop registration natively.

The tradeoff is throughput — sole-prop brands are capped low. For five local
clients sending review requests and missed-call replies, that ceiling is not
close to binding. Revisit when you're past roughly eight clients or start
sending bulk campaigns; that's the point to form the LLC, get the EIN, and
re-register as a Standard brand.

Form the LLC when you have revenue to protect. Not before. It costs $50–500
depending on state and solves a problem you don't have yet.

### One thing you *do* need immediately

CAN-SPAM requires a **valid physical postal address in every commercial
email**. Your options are a street address, a USPS-registered PO Box, or a
CMRA-registered private mailbox. Your home address technically qualifies but
publishing it to 200 strangers is a bad trade — get a PO Box or virtual
mailbox in week 1.

This is also the most commonly omitted compliance element, and Gmail and
Outlook read a complete footer as a legitimacy signal. Omitting it hurts
deliverability on top of being illegal.

The discipline that matters more than any line item: **sell first, build
second.** Do not start the GoHighLevel meter until someone has paid you.

automation platform. Anything promising clients on autopilot — if it worked,
they'd use it themselves rather than sell it to you.

## 5. THE 90 DAYS

### Weeks 1–2 — setup and list

- [ ] Domain + Google Workspace. Sending address on a **real domain**, never
      Gmail.
- [ ] PO Box / virtual mailbox ordered (CAN-SPAM footer blocker).
- [ ] A2P **sole proprietor** brand registered — before any EIN exists.
- [ ] Warm the sending domain: 10 emails/day week 1, 20/day week 2. Ramping
      straight to 50 gets you filtered permanently.
- [ ] Pull 200 local home-service businesses from Google Maps.
- [ ] Score every one (§6). Keep the top 100.
- [ ] Build the demo in the GHL free trial using a fake business. Record a
      90-second screen capture of a missed call producing a text.

The demo is the single highest-leverage asset here. Owners do not buy
descriptions of automation. They buy watching a phone buzz.

### Weeks 3–6 — outreach

- [ ] 20 touches/day: cold email, phone follow-up 48h later.
- [ ] Call between **7:30–9am or 4–6pm.** Mid-day they're under a sink.
- [ ] Target 3–5 discovery calls/week.
- [ ] Log every objection verbatim. After 30 calls the list is finite and
      you'll have an answer for all of it.

Expect roughly: 100 emails → 5–10 replies → 3–5 calls → 1 client. If you're
well off that pace after 100, the problem is the list, not the copy — you're
emailing businesses that don't have the pain.

### Weeks 6–9 — client #1

- [ ] Close one, founding-client rate, setup waived.
- [ ] Deliver hard. Onboard in under a week.
- [ ] **Screenshot the baseline before you turn anything on.** Review count,
      star average, date. Without a before, there is no after, and the case
      study is the actual product of this phase.
- [ ] Week 3 of service: collect the testimonial while enthusiasm is highest.

### Weeks 9–13 — leverage

- [ ] Case study becomes the entire pitch. Subject line stops being a
      question and becomes a number.
- [ ] Full price: $500 setup + $400/mo.
- [ ] Ask client #1 for two referrals. Home-service owners know every other
      trade in town — this is the cheapest channel you will ever have.
- [ ] Target 4–5 clients: **$1,600–2,000/mo recurring.**

## 6. LEAD SCORING

Pull from Google Maps. One point each — call anything scoring 3+:

- [ ] Under 30 Google reviews
- [ ] Most recent review older than 3 months
- [ ] Star average 3.8–4.5 (they care, and they're losing to a 4.9 competitor)
- [ ] No website, or one that's visibly a decade old
- [ ] No online booking
- [ ] Owner's name in the business name (owner-operator = decision maker
      answers the phone)
- [ ] 1–10 employees

**Skip:** franchises, anything with a marketing department, 4.9 stars with
500+ reviews (no pain), and under 5 reviews total (too small to pay).

The score is a proxy for one question: *is this person losing money in a way
they can already feel?* You are not creating awareness of a problem. You are
attaching a number to one they already have.

## 7. HOW THIS FAILS

Ranked by likelihood, from what actually kills people at this:

1. **Stopping outreach after ~40 emails because it "isn't working."** The
   math needs 100+ before it says anything. This is the number one killer and
   it isn't close.
2. **Building before selling.** Perfecting the GHL setup is procrastination
   that feels like work. The demo needs to be good enough to record, not
   good enough to ship.
3. **Paying for tools with no clients.** $97/mo against $0 revenue for three
   months is your entire budget, gone, with nothing learned.
4. **Discounting the monthly to close the first deal.** You'll spend the rest
   of the year anchored to it.
5. **Compliance shortcuts on reviews.** Incentives or gating will get the
   *client's* profile suspended. You will have destroyed a small business's
   primary lead source. See `COMPLIANCE.md` — this one has legal teeth, not
   just business consequences.
